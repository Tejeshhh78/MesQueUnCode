# The possession model

This is the technical description of the Mes Que Un Code entry: what it does, why, and every rule
it implements. Parameters live in [`solution/config.py`](../solution/config.py), each with a
comment giving its reason.

## The objective, and why spellF1 shapes everything

The board is ranked on **spellF1**: each possession (a maximal run of `H` or `A`) is one object,
matched one-to-one against the truth by temporal IoU, F1 averaged over IoU 0.3–0.7. Two
consequences drive the design:

1. **Flicker is catastrophic.** One wrong frame inside a 20-second possession splits it in two:
   the true spell loses its match (IoU < 0.5 for both halves) and a false spell appears. The
   nearest-player baseline scores macroF1 0.65 but spellF1 0.05 on the full match for this reason.
2. **Stoppages split possessions.** `H H D D H H` is two possessions. A spurious dead-ball blip
   costs as much as a spurious hand-over; a missed stoppage merges two possessions into one.

So the model is built around *segments*, not frames: per-frame evidence feeds a decoder that pays an
explicit price for every change of state.

## 1. Evidence

All evidence is a per-frame log-odds-like score; **zero means "no information"**, which is the
honest value whenever the ball is not seen.

### Team control — who could be playing the ball (`team[f]`, + favours H)

* **Ball track.** Gaps up to 10 frames (0.4 s) are linearly interpolated; longer gaps are left
  missing. Ball speed is a centred difference over ±2 frames.
* **Nearest player per team.** For each team, the distance from the ball to its nearest tracked
  player. Slots carry no identity, so nothing per-player is used — identity switches and
  duplicated tracks are harmless.
* **Control kernel.** `team = 0.35 · (k_H − k_A)` with `k = exp(−½ (d / 1.5 m)²)`. "Home 0.5 m,
  away 6 m" is strong evidence; "home 4 m, away 5 m" is almost none.
* **Ball in flight.** Above 9 m/s the evidence is multiplied by 0.15: a pass that flies past a
  defender has not been won by him (CHALLENGE.md: a deflection is not a change).
* **Ball out of play.** Team evidence is zeroed while the ball is past a line.
* **Smoothing.** Centred mean over ±6 frames (`--window`), so two players a few centimetres apart
  swapping "nearest" do not register.
* **Gaps.** Inside a long ball gap whose two ends disagree, a weak (×0.05) linear blend from the
  last evidence to the next. It only breaks the tie so a hidden hand-over is placed mid-gap.
* **Smart Tagging prior.** `BUILD UP`, `PROGRESSION` and `ATTACKING TRANSITION` intervals add
  ±0.03 per frame for their `Team`. We checked the tag semantics *without ground truth*: inside
  these intervals the nearest player to a visible ball belongs to the tagged team 71–87% of the
  time; `DEFENDING …` intervals are the same phases naming the defending team (13–38%) and are not
  used, to avoid counting each phase twice. `N/A` teams (every throw-in) contribute nothing.
* **Homography.** Where the tactical reprojection error exceeds 5 px (camera off the pitch, mostly
  around halftime), team evidence is halved.
* **Team shape (self-supervised).** On frames with no ball position only, a logistic regression on
  collective shape — width and length gaps between the teams, territory, forward movement over 1 s,
  all signed by each half's attacking direction from the ATD metadata — adds `0.02 × logit`. It is
  fitted on **silver labels** generated by a rule: one team's player within 1 m of a slow (<6 m/s)
  in-pitch ball and nobody from the other team within 2.5 m. Trained on one half and tested on the
  other it agrees with held-out silver labels 84% of the time (majority class 61–63%). The dominant
  cue is width: the team in possession stretches the pitch. No person labels anything.

### Dead ball (`dead[f]`, + favours D)

* **Ball out of the pitch** by more than 0.75 m: +0.6 per frame. A ball **last seen out stays out**
  through the following gap — the tracker very often loses the ball as it leaves the pitch.
* **Player motion.** Mean speed of tracked players, smoothed over ±1 s:
  `0.08 · tanh((1.4 − v) / 0.3)`. The centre and scale were calibrated on silver labels, not on
  ground truth: frames with the ball visibly out (dead) against frames with an in-pitch ball at
  pass speed (live). Their likelihood ratio crosses zero between 1.5 and 1.75 m/s; both silver sets
  are biased towards faster play than their class average, so the centre is taken a little lower.
  Frames with fewer than 12 tracked players give no motion evidence.
* **Ball placed for a restart.** Ball below 0.6 m/s for ≥1.5 s while players are slow: +0.25.
  Unknown player motion never counts as slow.
* **Moving ball.** An in-pitch ball above 4 m/s: −0.1 (play is live).
* **Set pieces.** The first 4 s of each Smart Tagging throw-in, corner, goal kick or free kick
  window: +0.15 per frame. A tip, not a decision — the tags are automatic.
* **Kick-offs.** In each `Kick Off` tag window, the first frame the ball leaves the centre spot
  (within 3 m, faster than 2 m/s) is the kick. Every frame of a half before its first kick-off is
  **forced** to `D`. This is what labels the 2,934 graded pre-kick-off frames of the second half.

## 2. Decoding (three-state Viterbi)

Per frame, scores for each state:

```text
score_H = +team/2 − dead/2 + alive_bias
score_A = −team/2 − dead/2 + alive_bias
score_D = +dead/2 + |team|/2
```

`D` receives `|team|/2` so that a player standing next to the ball says *whose* ball it would be,
not whether it is in play — the taker holds the ball at every throw-in. Alive versus dead is then
decided by the dead evidence alone.

Transition costs (nats): hand-over `H↔A` = 4.0, ball goes dead = 3.0, play restarts = 3.0. Viterbi
finds, independently in each half, the label sequence maximising total score minus transition
costs — the optimal segmentation under a per-change penalty. Properties that follow:

* **Hysteresis, exactly.** A hand-over needs ~11 frames (0.45 s) of unambiguous control by the new
  team, more when evidence is mixed. A touch or a lost aerial duel does not pay for it.
* **Loose balls stay with the last team.** Zero evidence leaves the current state unchanged.
* **No lag.** Viterbi is non-causal: the change is placed where the evidence turns, not after the
  penalty has been paid.
* **Change through a stoppage** (throw-in to the other side) is priced as dead + restart, not as a
  hand-over.
* Each half starts in `D`.

## 3. Clean-up

* Possession spells shorter than 1.0 s are absorbed: between two runs of the other team they
  become that team; next to one run of the other team they join it; between stoppages they become
  `D`. Shortest first, repeated until none remain.
* Dead-ball runs shorter than 1.0 s between two runs of the *same* team are bridged — `H D H` would
  otherwise count as two possessions.
* Graded frames are always `H`, `A` or `D`; ungraded frames are `X`.

## Possession rules as implemented

| situation | rule |
|---|---|
| loose ball, contested ball | stays with the last team (zero evidence carries the state) |
| deflection, single touch, aerial duel without control | no change unless the new team accumulates ≥ the hand-over cost |
| pass in flight past an opponent | evidence ×0.15 above 9 m/s |
| ball out of the pitch | D from the moment it crosses (+0.75 m), and while unseen afterwards |
| restart | D until the ball is played; the restart team is whoever then controls it |
| kick-off | D before it (forced); possession to the kicking team |
| goalkeeper holding the ball | ordinary control by the goalkeeper's team |
| halftime, before/after the match | X (ungraded) |
| ball invisible | carry the current state; team shape and tag priors may move it |
| dead-ball ownership | never asked: D |

## Why not something else

* **A per-frame classifier** (gradient boosting, a neural net) has no notion of the cost of a split
  and would need smoothing on top anyway — and there are no legitimate labels on this game to train
  it with. The rules forbid hand labels on the provided game and tuning on the public window.
* **Deep learning on video** cannot be trained here without labels on other games, and the
  tracking already gives positions.
* **The broadcast feed** needs its own video synchronisation, whose errors would land on transition
  timing; the tactical tracking covers every graded frame.

## Configuration and reproducibility

```bash
python solution/run_solution.py --print-params            # effective parameters as JSON
python solution/run_solution.py --set switch_cost=6        # override any parameter
python solution/run_solution.py --window 12                # evidence smoothing half-width
python solution/run_solution.py --no-clean                 # skip clean-up (ablation)
```

Parameter values were set from football and physics priors and from unlabelled statistics of this
match (distributions, silver labels). **None was chosen by scoring against the public 5-minute
ground truth.** The pipeline is deterministic; the same data and parameters give the same bytes.

## Known limitations and future work

* **Too few, too long possessions.** The full match decodes to 202 possessions of mean 18.9 s.
  Football priors suggest more and shorter, and the public 5 minutes agree (11 predicted against 17
  true). We have deliberately *not* lowered `switch_cost` to fix this on 17 possessions: EVALUATION.md
  shows that tuning on the public window picks the wrong answer. A principled fix needs labelled
  data from other matches.
* **Hand-overs hidden in ball gaps.** With the ball missing for runs of 10 s and more, an A→H→A
  sequence inside one gap is invisible to everything but the weak team-shape prior.
* **Dead-ball detection** without a referee signal relies on proxies; fouls in open play (ball stays
  in the pitch, players slow down gradually) are the hardest case.
* **Restart team** is whoever controls the ball first after the restart; a long throw that is
  immediately contested may be attributed to the wrong side.
* **Future work:** labelled tracking from *other* games to learn the emission model; a hidden
  semi-Markov decoder with explicit duration distributions; ball-trajectory re-detection in the
  tactical video for the 41% of missing frames; broadcast-to-tactical synchronisation to recover
  ball positions in close-ups.
